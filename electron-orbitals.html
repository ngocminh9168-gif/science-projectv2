Electron Orbitals

Explore the quantum mechanical model of atoms

What Are Electron Orbitals?

An electron orbital is the region of space around an atom's nucleus where an electron is likely to be found. Unlike the classical picture of electrons orbiting like planets, quantum mechanics tells us that electrons don't follow definite paths. Instead, they exist in probabilistic clouds called orbitals.

Orbitals are three-dimensional regions where there's a high probability of finding an electron. The size and shape of an orbital depend on the energy level of the electron.

Energy Levels and Shells

Electrons in atoms occupy different energy levels, also called electron shells. Each shell can hold a maximum number of electrons:

  • First shell (n=1): Holds up to 2 electrons (closest to nucleus, lowest energy)
  • Second shell (n=2): Holds up to 8 electrons
  • Third shell (n=3): Holds up to 18 electrons
  • Fourth shell and beyond: Hold increasingly more electrons

Electrons in lower energy levels (closer to the nucleus) are more tightly bound to the atom. Electrons fill energy levels from lowest to highest, following specific rules called the Aufbau principle.

Types of Orbitals

s Orbitals

S orbitals are spherical in shape and can hold a maximum of 2 electrons. They are the most common and have the lowest energy. The first shell has one s orbital (1s), the second shell has one s orbital (2s), and so on.

p Orbitals

P orbitals are dumbbell-shaped and can hold a maximum of 6 electrons (3 orbitals × 2 electrons each). P orbitals start appearing in the second shell (2p) and have higher energy than s orbitals in the same shell.

d Orbitals

D orbitals are more complex cloverleaf shapes and can hold a maximum of 10 electrons (5 orbitals × 2 electrons each). D orbitals start appearing in the third shell (3d).

f Orbitals

F orbitals are the most complex shapes and can hold a maximum of 14 electrons (7 orbitals × 2 electrons each). F orbitals start appearing in the fourth shell (4f).

The Quantum Number System

Electron orbitals are described by quantum numbers that specify the energy, shape, and orientation of the orbital:

  • Principal quantum number (n): Determines the energy level and size of the orbital (n = 1, 2, 3, ...)
  • Angular momentum quantum number (l): Determines the shape of the orbital (s, p, d, or f)
  • Magnetic quantum number (ml): Determines the orientation of the orbital in space
  • Spin quantum number (ms): Describes the spin of the electron (up or down)

Electron Configuration

The electron configuration of an atom describes how electrons are distributed among its orbitals. This is crucial for understanding an element's chemical properties. For example:

  • Hydrogen (H): 1s¹
  • Carbon (C): 1s² 2s² 2p²
  • Oxygen (O): 1s² 2s² 2p⁴
  • Iron (Fe): 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ 4s²

Why Orbitals Matter

Understanding electron orbitals is essential for explaining:

  • How atoms bond with other atoms
  • The chemical properties of elements
  • How atoms emit or absorb light
  • The arrangement of the periodic table
  • Chemical reactions and molecular structure
