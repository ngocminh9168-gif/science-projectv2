Atomic Structure

Understand the fundamental building blocks that make up all matter

What is an Atom?

An atom is the smallest unit of matter that retains the properties of a chemical element. It is the basic building block of all matter in the universe. Every solid, liquid, and gas you see is made up of atoms or groups of atoms bonded together.

The word "atom" comes from the Greek word "atomos," meaning "uncuttable" or "indivisible." Ancient philosophers theorized about atoms, but it wasn't until the early 1800s that scientists proved their existence experimentally.

The Three Main Parts of an Atom

1. The Nucleus

The nucleus is the dense, positively charged center of an atom. It contains most of the atom's mass, despite being incredibly small compared to the overall size of the atom. The nucleus contains two types of particles:

  • Protons: Positively charged particles that define what element an atom is. Each element has a unique number of protons.
  • Neutrons: Neutral particles (no charge) that add to the atom's mass. Different isotopes of the same element have different numbers of neutrons.

2. Electrons

Electrons are negatively charged particles that orbit around the nucleus in electron shells or energy levels. They are much smaller and lighter than protons and neutrons. Electrons determine how atoms interact chemically with other atoms.

3. The Electron Cloud

Rather than orbiting like planets, electrons exist in a "cloud" or orbital around the nucleus. This region is called the electron cloud or electron shell, and it determines the size and chemical properties of the atom.

Key Atomic Numbers

Atomic Number

The atomic number is the number of protons in an atom's nucleus. This number uniquely identifies an element. For example, all hydrogen atoms have 1 proton, all carbon atoms have 6 protons, and all oxygen atoms have 8 protons.

Mass Number

The mass number is the total number of protons and neutrons in an atom. It determines the atom's mass. Different isotopes of the same element have different mass numbers because they have different numbers of neutrons.

Atomic Mass

The atomic mass is the average mass of atoms of an element, taking into account the different isotopes and their abundance in nature.

How Atoms are Organized

In a neutral atom, the number of protons equals the number of electrons. This makes the atom electrically balanced. The electrons are arranged in shells around the nucleus, with each shell able to hold a certain maximum number of electrons:

  • First shell: up to 2 electrons
  • Second shell: up to 8 electrons
  • Third shell: up to 18 electrons

The chemical properties of an element are determined primarily by the electrons in the outermost shell, called the valence electrons.

Atomic Size

Atoms are incredibly small! A typical atom has a diameter of about 0.1 to 0.5 nanometers (a nanometer is one billionth of a meter). To put this in perspective, about 100 million atoms could fit in a single grain of sand. The nucleus is even smaller, about 100,000 times smaller than the atom itself!
