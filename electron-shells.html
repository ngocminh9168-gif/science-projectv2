Electron Shells

How electrons are arranged around the nucleus

What are Electron Shells?

Electron shells (also called energy levels or orbitals) are regions around the nucleus where electrons are found. Electrons are arranged in these shells, with each shell able to hold a maximum number of electrons. The arrangement of electrons in shells determines the chemical properties of an element.

Shell Capacities (Simple Model)

In the simple model used for GCSE Science:

First shell (Shell 1): Maximum 2 electrons
Second shell (Shell 2): Maximum 8 electrons
Third shell (Shell 3): Maximum 8 electrons

Electrons fill the first shell before moving to the second, and the second before the third (Aufbau principle).

Electron Configuration Notation

We write electron configurations as numbers representing electrons in each shell, from innermost to outermost.

Examples:

Hydrogen (H): 1 electron → 1
Carbon (C): 6 electrons → 2,4
Oxygen (O): 8 electrons → 2,6
Sodium (Na): 11 electrons → 2,8,1
Chlorine (Cl): 17 electrons → 2,8,7
Argon (Ar): 18 electrons → 2,8,8

Valence Electrons

The valence electrons are the electrons in the outermost shell. These electrons determine how an atom bonds with other atoms and its chemical reactivity.

Examples of Valence Electrons:

  • Carbon (2,4): 4 valence electrons
  • Oxygen (2,6): 6 valence electrons
  • Sodium (2,8,1): 1 valence electron
  • Chlorine (2,8,7): 7 valence electrons

Electron Shells and Reactivity

Atoms tend to react to achieve a full outer shell (stable configuration). Noble gases like argon (2,8,8) have full outer shells and are very unreactive.

Electron Shell Stability:

  • Full outer shell: Stable, unreactive (noble gases)
  • 1-3 electrons in outer shell: Likely to lose electrons (metals)
  • 5-7 electrons in outer shell: Likely to gain electrons (nonmetals)

Working Out Electron Configuration

Step 1: Find the Atomic Number

The atomic number tells you how many electrons the atom has.

Step 2: Fill Shells in Order

Fill the first shell with up to 2 electrons, then the second with up to 8, then the third with up to 8.

Example: Magnesium (Atomic Number 12)

First shell: 2 electrons (filled)
Second shell: 8 electrons (filled)
Third shell: 2 electrons (remaining)
Configuration: 2,8,2

Why This Matters for Chemistry

  • Electron configuration determines bonding behavior
  • Explains periodic table patterns and element properties
  • Determines ion formation and charges
  • Affects electronegativity and reactivity
  • Explains why certain elements react together
