Elements & Compounds

Understanding pure substances, mixtures, and separation methods

What are Elements?

An element is a pure substance made of only one type of atom. Each element has a unique atomic number (number of protons). Elements cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means.

Examples of Elements:

Carbon (C) - Atomic number 6
Oxygen (O) - Atomic number 8
Hydrogen (H) - Atomic number 1
Sodium (Na) - Atomic number 11
Chlorine (Cl) - Atomic number 17

What are Compounds?

A compound is a pure substance made of two or more elements chemically bonded together. Compounds have fixed composition and can be broken down into their constituent elements.

Properties of Compounds

  • Different from the properties of their individual elements
  • Have fixed melting and boiling points
  • Cannot be separated by physical methods
  • Have a definite chemical formula

Examples of Compounds:

Water (H₂O) - 2 hydrogen, 1 oxygen
Carbon dioxide (CO₂) - 1 carbon, 2 oxygen
Sodium chloride (NaCl) - 1 sodium, 1 chlorine
Ammonia (NH₃) - 1 nitrogen, 3 hydrogen
Methane (CH₄) - 1 carbon, 4 hydrogen

What are Mixtures?

A mixture is made of two or more pure substances (elements or compounds) that are not chemically bonded. The components retain their original properties and can be separated by physical methods.

Properties of Mixtures

  • Components can be in any proportion
  • No new substance is formed
  • Properties depend on the proportion of components
  • Can be separated by physical methods

Examples of Mixtures:

  • Salt water (salt and water)
  • Sand and gravel (different particles)
  • Air (nitrogen, oxygen, argon, etc.)
  • Seawater (water and dissolved salts)

Separation Methods for Mixtures

Filtration

Separates insoluble solids from liquids using filter paper. The solid is retained while the liquid passes through.

  • Used for: Sand and water, soil and water
  • When to use: Solid particles are large enough to be trapped

Evaporation

Heats a solution to evaporate the solvent (usually water), leaving the dissolved solute behind as crystals or powder.

  • Used for: Salt from seawater, sugar from solution
  • When to use: Solute is non-volatile (doesn't easily evaporate)

Crystallization

A more controlled form of evaporation that produces pure crystals of the solute by slowly cooling a hot saturated solution.

  • Used for: Copper sulfate crystals, salt crystals
  • When to use: Need to obtain pure crystals

Chromatography

Separates components of a mixture based on their ability to dissolve in a moving solvent. Different substances travel different distances.

  • Used for: Plant pigments, dyes, amino acids
  • When to use: Need to separate similar soluble substances

Distillation

Separates components based on different boiling points. The mixture is heated to evaporate, then the vapor is condensed back to a liquid.

  • Used for: Separating crude oil, obtaining pure water from saltwater
  • When to use: Components have significantly different boiling points

Magnetic Separation

Uses a magnet to separate magnetic materials from non-magnetic materials in a mixture.

  • Used for: Iron filings and sand, recycling metals
  • When to use: One component is magnetic

Key Points to Remember

  • Elements contain one type of atom
  • Compounds are chemically bonded elements
  • Mixtures are physically combined substances
  • Mixtures can be separated by physical methods
  • Different mixtures require different separation techniques
  • Pure substances have fixed properties
  • Mixtures can have variable properties depending on composition
